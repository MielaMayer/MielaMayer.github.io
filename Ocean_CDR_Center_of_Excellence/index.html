Ocean CDR {marine biogeochemistry}

Carbonate chemistry · alkalinity · biological pump · enhanced weathering

A structured map of marine carbon dioxide removal: the chemistry that actually moves carbon, the routes that claim to, and the labs and companies defining the field.

domain: ocean CDR / OAE / ERW / BCP · geo: global, coastal + EEZ + farmland runoff · horizon: 101–104 years · objective: geologic-scale, measurable, durable drawdown

How this knowledge space works

The spine — read this before the org chart

The atmosphere and the surface ocean share carbon dioxide. When CO2 dissolves it does not stay a gas in the drink. It reacts. Most of it becomes bicarbonate (HCO3−). A smaller share becomes carbonate (CO32−). Together with leftover dissolved CO2 they are DIC — dissolved inorganic carbon. The ocean already holds on the order of forty to fifty times more carbon than the air, almost all as those ions.

Alkalinity (often written TA, total alkalinity) is not “how much carbon.” It is how many protons (H+) the water can soak up. Bicarbonate is worth one unit, carbonate two, hydroxide one. Sulfate is doubly charged and worth zero, because at seawater pH it will not take a proton. Charge ≠ drawdown.

Add base — dissolve olivine or basalt, dose magnesium hydroxide, run an electrochemical cell, dissolve limestone in high-CO2 wastewater — and you consume protons. The carbonate equilibrium shifts: dissolved CO2 becomes bicarbonate. The surface is then short of CO2 relative to the air, so more CO2 crosses in. That extra carbon sits as HCO3− for ~10,000 years if you do not let it crash out as rock. That is ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE). Enhanced rock weathering (ERW) is the same chemistry started on a field; rain and soil acids do the dissolving; rivers deliver the bicarbonate.

Build a shell and you run the reaction backwards. Calcite (CaCO3) takes two bicarbonates and keeps one carbon; the other leaves as CO2. Calcification from seawater alkalinity is a CO2 source per mole of shell, or at best a half-efficient durable sink if you insist on counting the carbon left in the mineral.

Photosynthesis is a third ledger. It builds POC — particulate organic carbon, soft tissue. That carbon is only “removed” until something eats it. PIC — particulate inorganic carbon is the mineral particles (coccoliths, shell hash). PIC:POC is how shell-heavy a cell or a bloom is. The biological carbon pump is the natural process that sends some POC (and PIC ballast) downward as marine snow. Most of it is respired in the top few hundred meters. Enhancing that pump means competing for the same nutrients the food web already uses, and then proving a stranger’s carbon actually arrived at depth.

The sorting question for every lab and company: after the intervention, where is the extra carbon, in what molecule, and how would a stranger measure that it is still there a year later?

Paradigm shifts · Step 1

  • Store as rock → store as dissolved bicarbonate — precipitation halves efficiency; DIC for ~104 yr is “durable enough” for climate, even if it offends a geologist’s sense of forever.
  • Grow biomass = sink → biomass is a leaky inventory — the climate product is permanence + MRV, not wet tons of algae.
  • Open-ocean fertilization as the big lever → point-source alkalinity and farmland ERW — outfalls, wastewater, existing tractors beat mid-ocean science projects on measurability.
  • Credits will fund Gt scale → philanthropy + public MRV + offtakes like Frontier, not a carbon coin — verification is the scarce asset that any new entrant has to solve before revenue.
  • Building objects won’t scale / growing will → the constraint is chemistry and proof, not whether the actuator is a cell or a mill — ERW already uses farm equipment; calcification “grows” a CO2 source.

Carbonate chemistry, with pictures

CO₂ · TA +0 Linear O=C=O. Exchanges with air. Neutral — no alkalinity.
HCO₃⁻ · TA +1 · ~90% of DIC The workhorse store. One proton acceptor.
CO₃²⁻ · TA +2 Pulls CO₂ from air. Too much + Ca²⁺ → calcite crashes out.
OH⁻ · TA +1 What lime and electrochemical OAE actually deliver.
SO₄²⁻ / Cl⁻ · TA +0 Abundant spectators. Proof that “negative ion” ≠ alkalinity.
CaCO₃ calcite Limestone, chalk, marble, shells, coccoliths. One C in the lattice.
CO₂(air) ⇌ CO₂(aq) + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ ⇌ 2H⁺ + CO₃²⁻

Olivine (ideal): Mg₂SiO₄ + 4 CO₂ + 4 H₂O → 2 Mg²⁺ + 4 HCO₃⁻ + H₄SiO₄

Lime: Ca(OH)₂ + 2 CO₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2 HCO₃⁻

Limestone in high-CO₂ water (Crew): CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O → Ca²⁺ + 2 HCO₃⁻

Calcification / unwanted precipitation: Ca²⁺ + 2 HCO₃⁻ → CaCO₃ + CO₂ + H₂O
basalt = lava rock (plagioclase + pyroxene + olivine) olivine = green sand (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄ brucite = Mg(OH)₂ slaked lime = Ca(OH)₂ quicklime = CaO (making it emits CO₂)

Numbers: cropland ERW potential ~0.5–2 Gt CO₂/yr by 2050 (Beerling et al. 2020) · OAE plausible Gt-scale with siting caveats · DIC lifetime ~10⁴ yr · wastewater CO₂ up to ~100× atmospheric equilibrium · human emissions ~40 Gt/yr.

Routes — four sectors, one ledger

SectorActuatorCarbon’s moleculeFailure mode
OAEAdd alkali to seawaterHCO₃⁻Overshoot → CaCO₃ precipitates; half the tons vanish as CO₂
ERWCrush silicate on fieldsHCO₃⁻ via riversDid it dissolve, or is the dust still sitting there?
Biological pumpGrow / ballast plankton or kelpPOC (± PIC)Eaten; nutrients stolen; unmeasured export
Calcifier materialsGrow CaCO₃ on waste alkalisolid carbonate productAlkali must be imported; market is not Gt

Coccolithophores sit on the joint of pump + calcification. Photosynthesis writes POC. Plates write PIC and vent CO₂. They only help as (a) ballast for export you can prove, or (b) a harvested mineral whose alkalinity came from waste, not seawater. There is no third path that makes seawater-fed shell growth a full sink.

Industry · Step 3

Established adopters / buyers

  • Frontier — $1.8B advance market commitment across Stripe, Alphabet, Meta, Shopify, McKinsey, and (from 2026) Anthropic, Google, Salesforce and H&M. The serious offtake.
  • Microsoft, Stripe, Shopify historical CDR buyers — set early OAE/ERW contracts.
  • Wastewater utilities (Crew’s channel) and coastal outfalls (Planetary’s channel) — existing pipes.
  • Row-crop agriculture — the ERW “factory” already exists as spreaders.

Prime movers

  • Planetary — Mg(OH)₂ at outfalls. Point-source OAE.
  • Ebb Carbon — electrochemical alkalinity from seawater.
  • Crew Carbon — limestone + wastewater (up to ~100× atmospheric CO₂ concentration). Yale spinout (Katchinoff/Planavsky).
  • Lithos — basalt on US farmland. Planavsky/Reinhard lab lineage.
  • Mati Carbon (India), Terradot (Brazil) — same ERW play, tropics. Mati won the $50M XPRIZE Carbon Removal grand prize in 2025.

Disruptors / contested

  • Vesta — coastal olivine. Dissolution + Ni/Cr release is a monitored, open concern; early site data show no accumulation yet.
  • GigaBlue — mineral-ballasted marine snow. Not OAE. MRV of arrival is the hole. See the deep dive for a full case study.
  • Marnova — kelp co-settled with calcifying-organism larvae as ballast, aiming for export past the thermocline. Early-stage; mechanism not independently verified.
  • Bloomineral — algae-driven biomineralization: waste alkalinity (demolition/soda-ash/sugar-industry waste) converted to CaCO₃ for paint, cement and polymer products.

Centers of excellence & organizations · Step 3

  • Yale Center for Natural Carbon Capture (YCNCC) — natural carbon capture and enhanced weathering research; Noah Planavsky geochem group among its affiliated labs. Field partners wanted.
  • Ocean Visions — the map (their mCDR roadmap covers export/avoided-degradation holes). Coordinating with EDF on a phytoplankton carbon research program (RFPs expected late 2026/early 2027).
  • Carbon to Sea Initiative — $5M "Global OAE Field Research Network" RFP plus a $1M targeted research track, part of a larger $50M+ effort backed by Additional Ventures.
  • ORCA (Ocean Resilience and Climate Alliance) — $300M+ philanthropic coalition (Bloomberg Philanthropies, Rockefeller Brothers Fund, and others) where marine CDR is one of seven priorities.
  • Renaissance Philanthropy — climate-funding intermediary active in the broader carbon-removal philanthropy space.
  • GEOMAR, PML, WHOI, MBARI, University of Exeter, Heriot-Watt, Dalhousie — classical marine carbonate labs; Dalhousie and GEOMAR co-run the "Ocean Alk-Align" OAE project, PML and Exeter co-lead SeaCURE and related OAE workshops.
  • Standards / methods: best-practice OAE guides (Ocean Visions, NASEM ocean CDR), new PIC:POC protocol (this year).

Core competencies & skills · Step 2

  • Seawater carbonate system (DIC, TA, pH, pCO₂, speciation) and the Revelle factor
  • Silicate weathering kinetics (olivine vs basalt vs limestone)
  • Biological pump: export efficiency, Martin curve, ballast hypothesis, PIC:POC
  • MRV design: tracers, mixing models, air–sea flux, additionality
  • Tools: coulometric DIC, open-cell TA titration, GC-MS, ICP-MS for cations, sat imagery for blooms
  • Feedstock logistics: slag, demolition fines, mine tailings vs coasts and cement plants
  • Honesty about scale: Gt math, not demo-tank math

MRV is the product

  • OAE / ERW: alkali added, cations released, extra DIC after mixing and air–sea exchange. Never count precipitated CaCO₃ as a full tonne.
  • Bio export: flux at depth, remineralization profile, additionality vs the natural pump. No cheap trusted meter. GigaBlue lives or dies here.
  • Materials: mass of product − process emissions − origin of the alkali.

A modest research budget can buy a closed-tank mass balance that shows the 2 HCO₃⁻ → CO₂ vent, a PIC:POC strain comparison under the new protocol, or a public waste-alkali map. It cannot buy “we learned to grow coccolithophores at climate scale.”
